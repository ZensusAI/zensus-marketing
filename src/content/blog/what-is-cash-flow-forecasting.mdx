<p id="blog-article-lead" className="text-lg leading-relaxed text-muted-foreground">Cash flow forecasting is estimating how much cash will come into and go out of a business over the coming weeks or months, so you can see when cash gets tight before it happens.</p>


<p>A profit and loss statement tells you whether you made money. A cash flow forecast tells you whether you will have money in the bank on the day payroll, rent, and vendor bills are due. The two disagree whenever customers pay late, contracts bill annually, or a big expense lands in the same week as a payroll run.</p>


<p className="mb-2 font-medium text-foreground">A useful cash flow forecast answers three questions:</p>


<ul>

  <li>How much cash will be in the bank each week or month ahead?</li>
  <li>When is the lowest point, and does it stay above the cash floor you need?</li>
  <li>What changes that low point: a new hire, a late customer, a lost contract?</li>

</ul>

## What Is Cash Flow Forecasting?


<p>Cash flow forecasting is the process of estimating future cash inflows and outflows over a specific period, dated by when the money actually moves.</p>


<p className="mb-2 font-medium text-foreground">A forecast is built from the cash you expect to receive and pay:</p>


<ul>

  <li>customer payments and subscription renewals</li>
  <li>payroll, on its pay dates</li>
  <li>vendor bills, rent, and software</li>
  <li>taxes and loan repayments</li>
  <li>capital expenditures and financing</li>

</ul>

  height={600}
  alt="Diagram of company cash inflows from sales revenue, investments, and funding, and outflows for operating expenses, payroll, taxes, and vendor payments"
  caption="Cash flow forecasting models how money moves into and out of your business over time."
/>


<p>It starts from the fact that profit and cash are different things. The SEC's <a href="https://www.sec.gov/about/reports-publications/investorpubsbegfinstmtguide" className="font-medium text-primary underline-offset-4 hover:underline">beginners' guide to financial statements</a> puts it this way: "While an income statement can tell you whether a company made a profit, a cash flow statement can tell you whether the company generated cash." A forecast applies the same idea to the weeks ahead.</p>


## Why Is Cash Flow Forecasting Important?


<p>Cash flow forecasting is important because profit and growth do not show whether there will be cash in the bank on the day payroll and bills are due. A forecast shows that date in advance.</p>


<p>Strong revenue does not guarantee healthy cash flow. In the Federal Reserve's <a href="https://www.fedsmallbusiness.org/reports/survey/2025/2025-report-on-employer-firms" className="font-medium text-primary underline-offset-4 hover:underline">2024 Small Business Credit Survey</a>, 75% of employer firms named rising costs as a financial challenge, 56% named paying operating expenses, and 51% named uneven cash flows.</p>


<p className="mb-2 font-medium text-foreground">A forecast turns those risks into dates you can plan around:</p>


<ul>

  <li>the week a <a href="/blog/what-happens-if-you-miss-payroll" className="font-medium text-primary underline-offset-4 hover:underline">payroll run</a> would not clear</li>
  <li>how long a late customer can stay late before you need a credit line</li>
  <li>whether you can afford a hire this quarter or next</li>
  <li>how many months of <a href="/blog/runway-vs-burn-rate-for-founders" className="font-medium text-primary underline-offset-4 hover:underline">runway</a> you have at today's burn rate</li>

</ul>


<p>It is also hard to do well. The Association for Financial Professionals (AFP) <a href="https://www.financialprofessionals.org/glossary/cash-forecasting" className="font-medium text-primary underline-offset-4 hover:underline">writes</a>: "Cash forecasting remains one of treasury's more challenging processes, in large part because it hasn't been entirely automated. Most treasurers conduct their forecast manually, collecting some information directly from ERPs and the rest from other departments and bank accounts."</p>


## How Does Cash Flow Forecasting Work?


<p>A forecast combines what has already happened with what is scheduled to happen. It starts from today's cleared bank balance, adds the money expected in, subtracts the money committed out, and rolls the balance forward one period at a time.</p>


<p>It is then updated as actuals arrive. Each week, real receipts and payments replace the estimates, and the forecast looks one period further ahead.</p>

## How Do You Build a Cash Flow Forecast?


<p className="mb-2 font-medium text-foreground">A direct, weekly cash flow forecast takes seven steps:</p>


<ol>

  <li><strong>Pick the horizon and the period.</strong> Thirteen weeks, by week, suits most operating decisions.</li>
  <li><strong>Start from today's bank balance.</strong> Use what has cleared, not what the books say you are owed.</li>
  <li><strong>List expected inflows by the week they will arrive.</strong> Open invoices, subscription renewals, and any financing, dated by when the customer actually pays.</li>
  <li><strong>List committed outflows by the week they leave.</strong> Payroll on its pay dates, rent, loan payments, taxes, and vendor bills on their due dates.</li>
  <li><strong>Roll the balance forward.</strong> Each week's closing balance is the next week's opening balance.</li>
  <li><strong>Mark the low point against a cash floor.</strong> Many teams use at least one payroll run as the floor.</li>
  <li><strong>Replace estimates with actuals every week, and add a new week at the end.</strong> That is what makes it a rolling forecast.</li>

</ol>


<p>The Excel version of these steps, with the formulas, is in <a href="/blog/how-to-build-a-13-week-cash-flow-forecast-in-excel" className="font-medium text-primary underline-offset-4 hover:underline">how to build a 13-week cash flow forecast in Excel</a>. Payroll dates are the line most often wrong by a week, and the <a href="/tools/payroll-calendar" className="font-medium text-primary underline-offset-4 hover:underline">2026 and 2027 payroll calendar</a> lists every one for your schedule.</p>

<p>Most businesses forecast 13 weeks ahead by week for operating decisions, and 12 to 18 months ahead by month for budgeting and planning.</p>

Include forecasted income statements, balance sheets, cash flow statements, and capital expenditure budgets."</p>


## What Is the Difference Between Direct and Indirect Cash Flow Forecasting?


<p>The direct method lists the cash you expect to receive and pay, date by date. The indirect method starts from projected profit and adjusts it for non-cash items and working capital.</p>


<p>The names come from cash flow reporting. The accounting standard <a href="https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-7-statement-of-cash-flows/" className="font-medium text-primary underline-offset-4 hover:underline">IAS 7</a> describes the direct method as one "whereby major classes of gross cash receipts and gross cash payments are disclosed", and the indirect method as one "whereby profit or loss is adjusted for the effects of transactions of a non-cash nature".</p>


<BlogTable
  caption="The direct and indirect methods side by side."
  columns={['', 'Direct method', 'Indirect method']}
  rows={[
    ['Starts from', 'Expected cash receipts and payments', 'Projected net income'],

    ['Typical period', 'Daily or weekly', 'Monthly or quarterly'],
    ['Best for', 'Short-term liquidity, payroll, the 13-week forecast', 'FP&A, strategic planning, long-term forecasts'],
  ]}
/>


<p>For the weeks ahead, use the direct method. It is the one that shows week 3 in the opening example.</p>


## What Is a Rolling Cash Flow Forecast?


<p>A rolling cash flow forecast keeps a fixed window ahead of you. A 13-week rolling forecast always shows the next 13 weeks: when a week closes, its actuals replace the estimates and a new week is added at the end.</p>


<p>That is the difference from an annual budget, which is set once and drifts further from reality every month. A business deciding whether to hire next quarter uses the rolling forecast, because it already includes this week's late payment and last week's new contract. For the full 13-week model, see <a href="/blog/what-is-a-13-week-cash-flow-forecast" className="font-medium text-primary underline-offset-4 hover:underline">What Is a 13-Week Cash Flow Forecast?</a></p>


## Should You Forecast Cash Flow Weekly or Monthly?


<p>A monthly forecast shows where the month ends. A weekly forecast shows the lowest point inside it, which is the number that decides whether payroll clears.</p>


<BlogTable
  caption="Weekly and monthly cash flow forecasts answer different questions; many teams run both."
  columns={['', 'Weekly forecast', 'Monthly forecast']}
  rows={[

    ['Updated', 'Every week, replacing estimates with actuals', 'Every month'],
    ['Best for', 'Payroll timing, late customers, tight cash', 'Budgets, hiring plans, board reporting'],

  ]}
/>


<p className="mb-2 font-medium text-foreground">Use a weekly forecast when:</p>


<ul>

  <li>payroll runs biweekly or weekly, so some months have three pay dates</li>
  <li>customers pay on 30 to 60 day terms, or often pay late</li>
  <li>revenue arrives in lumps: annual contracts, milestone billing, seasonal sales</li>
  <li>cash on hand covers less than about three months of spending</li>

</ul>


<p>A monthly forecast is enough for budgeting and for businesses whose cash arrives evenly. Many teams keep both: 13 weeks by week for operations, and 12 to 18 months by month for planning. The guide to the <a href="/blog/zero-cash-date-for-founders" className="font-medium text-primary underline-offset-4 hover:underline">zero cash date</a> works through a case where six months of monthly runway becomes four and a half.</p>


## How Do You Forecast Accounts Receivable?


<p>Accounts receivable is usually the least reliable line in a cash flow forecast, because the due date on an invoice is not the day the cash arrives.</p>


<p className="mb-2 font-medium text-foreground">To forecast receivables:</p>


<ul>

  <li>date each open invoice by when that customer usually pays, not by its due date</li>
  <li>move overdue invoices forward to the week you realistically expect them, rather than counting them as cash</li>
  <li>track days sales outstanding (DSO) and update the forecast when it rises</li>
  <li>for subscription revenue, place annual and quarterly payments on the dates they bill, not spread evenly across the year</li>
  <li>keep pipeline separate from booked revenue until a deal is signed and invoiced</li>

</ul>


<p>The <a href="/blog/cash-conversion-cycle" className="font-medium text-primary underline-offset-4 hover:underline">cash conversion cycle</a> guide explains DSO and how it ties up cash. For businesses whose clients pay late while payroll never waits, see <a href="/blog/cash-flow-forecasting-for-agencies" className="font-medium text-primary underline-offset-4 hover:underline">cash flow forecasting for agencies</a>. For turning CRM data into dated inflows, see <a href="/blog/hubspot-pipeline-to-cash-forecast" className="font-medium text-primary underline-offset-4 hover:underline">forecasting cash from HubSpot</a>.</p>


## What Is Predictive Cash Flow Forecasting?


<p>In treasury software, predictive cash flow forecasting usually means using payment history, and often statistical or machine-learning models, to predict when each invoice will actually be paid, then forecasting on those predicted dates instead of the due dates.</p>


<p>A small business gets most of that benefit without a model. Date each invoice by when that customer has paid before, compare the forecast with what actually happened every week, and adjust the customers who keep slipping. The guide to <a href="/blog/predictive-cash-flow-forecasting" className="font-medium text-primary underline-offset-4 hover:underline">predictive cash flow forecasting</a> works through it with dated examples and a one-hour starter forecast.</p>

## What Is Automated Cash Flow Forecasting?


<p>Automated cash flow forecasting builds the forecast from connected systems instead of exports. The bank, the accounting system, and the billing or CRM system feed the forecast directly, so balances, open invoices, and bills update when they change.</p>


<p className="mb-2 font-medium text-foreground">Automation takes over:</p>


<ul>

  <li>pulling bank balances and transactions</li>
  <li>pulling open invoices and bills from accounting</li>
  <li>placing subscription and contract payments on their dates</li>
  <li>rolling the forecast forward as actuals arrive</li>

</ul>


<p>It does not take over the judgment calls: which customers will pay late, which hires to make, and what cash floor is safe. Those stay with you as assumptions and scenarios.</p>

## What Are the Warning Signs of a Cash Flow Problem?


<p className="mb-2 font-medium text-foreground">A cash flow forecast is most useful when it warns you early. Watch for:</p>


<ul>

  <li>the projected balance dropping below your cash floor within the next 30 days</li>
  <li>days sales outstanding rising for two or more months in a row</li>
  <li>a month with three payroll runs, or a year with 27 biweekly pay dates</li>
  <li>a large customer whose payment has moved later more than once</li>
  <li>net burn rising while revenue is flat</li>

</ul>


<p>Each of these shows up in a weekly forecast weeks before it shows up in the bank balance. The guide to <a href="/blog/will-i-make-payroll" className="font-medium text-primary underline-offset-4 hover:underline">knowing whether you will make payroll</a> turns the first one into a checklist, and <a href="/blog/27-pay-periods-2026-2027" className="font-medium text-primary underline-offset-4 hover:underline">27 pay periods in 2026 or 2027</a> explains the extra payroll run. To turn these signs into thresholds and alerts, see how to set up a <a href="/blog/cash-flow-early-warning-system" className="font-medium text-primary underline-offset-4 hover:underline">cash flow early warning system</a>.</p>


## Why Do Cash Flow Forecasts Go Wrong?


<p>Most forecasts fail for one of three reasons, and each has a fix.</p>


<p className="mb-2 font-medium text-foreground">Where they go wrong:</p>


<ul>

  <li><strong>Spreadsheet errors.</strong> A formula that stops one row short, or a pasted value where a formula used to be. Raymond Panko's review of the research, <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/0802.3457" className="font-medium text-primary underline-offset-4 hover:underline">Spreadsheet Errors: What We Know. What We Think We Can Do</a>, opens: "Fifteen years of research studies have concluded unanimously that spreadsheet errors are both common and non-trivial." The fix is fewer manual steps and a check that each week's opening balance equals the last week's close.</li>
  <li><strong>Data from too many places.</strong> The bank balance comes from one login, open invoices from the accounting system, and annual renewals from the CRM. By the time all three are pasted in, one is out of date. The fix is connecting the sources rather than exporting them.</li>
  <li><strong>Due dates treated as payment dates.</strong> An invoice due on the 1st that is paid on the 20th moves cash three weeks, and in a tight month that is the whole cushion. The fix is dating receivables by how each customer actually pays.</li>

</ul>

## How Do You Measure Forecast Accuracy?


<p>Compare what the forecast said with what happened, every week, and track the difference.</p>


<p>Suppose the forecast said week 4 would close at $115,000 and it closed at $103,000 because one customer paid $12,000 less than expected. The variance is minus $12,000, or about 10% of the forecast. One week of that is noise. The same customer short three weeks running is a pattern, and the forecast for that customer should change.</p>


<p className="mb-2 font-medium text-foreground">Track variance on three lines:</p>


<ul>

  <li>the closing balance, which tells you how far off the whole forecast is</li>
  <li>customer receipts, usually the biggest source of error</li>
  <li>payroll and committed bills, which should be close to exact</li>

</ul>
