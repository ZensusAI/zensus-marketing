<p className="mb-2 font-medium text-foreground">A useful cash flow forecast answers three questions:</p>


<ul>

## What Is Cash Flow Forecasting?


<p>Cash flow forecasting is the process of estimating future cash inflows and outflows over a specific period of time.</p>


<p className="mb-2 font-medium text-foreground">A cash flow forecast helps businesses understand:</p>


<ul>

  <li>how much cash will be available</li>
  <li>when expenses are due</li>
  <li>whether liquidity levels are healthy</li>
  <li>how future financial obligations can be managed</li>

</ul>


<p>Forecasting gives finance leaders the visibility needed to make informed business decisions and maintain operational stability.</p>


<p className="mb-2 font-medium text-foreground">Typical forecast inputs include:</p>


<ul>

  <li>customer payments</li>
  <li>payroll expenses</li>
  <li>vendor payments</li>
  <li>taxes</li>
  <li>debt repayments</li>
  <li>operational expenses</li>
  <li>capital expenditures</li>

</ul>


<p>The primary objective is to predict future cash movement as accurately as possible.</p>

## How Do You Build a Cash Flow Forecast?


<p className="mb-2 font-medium text-foreground">A direct, weekly cash flow forecast takes seven steps:</p>


<ol>

  <li><strong>Pick the horizon and the period.</strong> Thirteen weeks, by week, suits most operating decisions.</li>
  <li><strong>Start from today's bank balance.</strong> Use what has cleared, not what the books say you are owed.</li>
  <li><strong>List expected inflows by the week they will arrive.</strong> Open invoices, subscription renewals, and any financing, dated by when the customer actually pays.</li>
  <li><strong>List committed outflows by the week they leave.</strong> Payroll on its pay dates, rent, loan payments, taxes, and vendor bills on their due dates.</li>
  <li><strong>Roll the balance forward.</strong> Each week's closing balance is the next week's opening balance.</li>
  <li><strong>Mark the low point against a cash floor.</strong> Many teams use at least one payroll run as the floor.</li>
  <li><strong>Replace estimates with actuals every week, and add a new week at the end.</strong> That is what makes it a rolling forecast.</li>

</ol>


<p>The Excel version of these steps, with the formulas, is in <a href="/blog/how-to-build-a-13-week-cash-flow-forecast-in-excel" className="font-medium text-primary underline-offset-4 hover:underline">how to build a 13-week cash flow forecast in Excel</a>. Payroll dates are the line most often wrong by a week, and the <a href="/tools/payroll-calendar" className="font-medium text-primary underline-offset-4 hover:underline">2026 and 2027 payroll calendar</a> lists every one for your schedule.</p>


## How Far Ahead Should a Cash Flow Forecast Go?


<p>Most businesses forecast 13 weeks ahead by week for operating decisions, and 12 to 18 months ahead by month for budgeting and planning.</p>


<p>Forecasts are grouped by how far ahead they look. The shorter the horizon, the more detail and the more often it is updated.</p>


### Short Term Forecasting


<p className="mb-2 font-medium text-foreground">Short term forecasts usually cover:</p>


<ul>

  <li>daily forecasting</li>
  <li>weekly forecasting</li>
  <li>monthly forecasting</li>

</ul>


<p>These forecasts are primarily used for operational liquidity management and treasury activities. The most common short-term horizon is 13 weeks: one quarter, by week, long enough to see a payroll gap or a slow-paying customer coming and short enough to forecast from invoices and bills you can already see. ### Medium Term Forecasting


<p className="mb-2 font-medium text-foreground">Medium term forecasts often cover:</p>


<ul>

  <li>quarterly forecasts</li>
  <li>six month forecasts</li>
  <li>annual planning periods</li>

</ul>


<p>These are commonly used for budgeting and operational planning.</p>

## What Is the Difference Between Direct and Indirect Cash Flow Forecasting?


<p>The direct method lists the cash you expect to receive and pay, date by date. The indirect method starts from projected profit and adjusts it for non-cash items and working capital. Different forecasting methods serve different business objectives.</p>


### Direct Cash Flow Forecasting


<p>The direct method forecasts actual expected cash receipts and payments. The name comes from cash flow reporting: the accounting standard <a href="https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-7-statement-of-cash-flows/" className="font-medium text-primary underline-offset-4 hover:underline">IAS 7</a> describes the direct method as one "whereby major classes of gross cash receipts and gross cash payments are disclosed".</p>


<p className="mb-2 font-medium text-foreground">This includes:</p>


<ul>

  <li>customer collections</li>
  <li>payroll expenses</li>
  <li>supplier payments</li>
  <li>rent and operational expenses</li>

</ul>


<p>Direct forecasting provides detailed short term visibility and is commonly used for treasury operations and liquidity management.</p>


### Indirect Cash Flow Forecasting


<p>IAS 7 describes the indirect method as one "whereby profit or loss is adjusted for the effects of transactions of a non-cash nature". A forecast built this way works the same way, from projected statements.</p>

<p className="mb-2 font-medium text-foreground">The indirect method starts with projected financial statements and adjusts for:</p>


<ul>

  <li>depreciation</li>
  <li>working capital changes</li>
  <li>non cash expenses</li>

</ul>


<p className="mb-2 font-medium text-foreground">This method is often used for:</p>


<ul>

  <li>FP&A processes</li>
  <li>strategic planning</li>
  <li>long term financial forecasting</li>

</ul>

## What Is a Rolling Cash Flow Forecast?


<p>A rolling cash flow forecast continuously updates as time progresses.</p>


<p>Instead of creating a fixed annual forecast, businesses continuously extend the forecasting window.</p>


<p className="mb-2 font-medium text-foreground">For example:</p>


<ul>

  <li>a 13 week cash flow forecast may update weekly</li>
  <li>once one week is completed, another week is added</li>

</ul>

<p>This allows finance teams to maintain continuous visibility into future liquidity. For example, a startup planning new hires may use a rolling forecast to determine whether projected cash reserves can support additional operational expenses over the next quarter.</p>


<p className="mb-2 font-medium text-foreground">Rolling forecasts have become increasingly popular because they improve:</p>


<ul>

  <li>forecast accuracy</li>
  <li>business agility</li>
  <li>responsiveness to changing conditions</li>

</ul>


## Should You Forecast Cash Flow Weekly or Monthly?


<p>A monthly forecast shows where the month ends. A weekly forecast shows the lowest point inside it, which is the number that decides whether payroll clears.</p>

<p className="mb-2 font-medium text-foreground">Use a weekly forecast when:</p>


<ul>

  <li>payroll runs biweekly or weekly, so some months have three pay dates</li>
  <li>customers pay on 30 to 60 day terms, or often pay late</li>
  <li>revenue arrives in lumps: annual contracts, milestone billing, seasonal sales</li>
  <li>cash on hand covers less than about three months of spending</li>

</ul>


<p>A monthly forecast is enough for budgeting and for businesses whose cash arrives evenly. Many teams keep both: 13 weeks by week for operations, and 12 to 18 months by month for planning. The opening example shows why: the same month ends at $115,000 monthly and bottoms out at $25,000 weekly. ## How Do You Forecast Accounts Receivable?


<p>Accounts receivable is usually the least reliable line in a cash flow forecast, because the due date on an invoice is not the day the cash arrives.</p>


<p className="mb-2 font-medium text-foreground">To forecast receivables:</p>


<ul>

  <li>date each open invoice by when that customer usually pays, not by its due date</li>
  <li>move overdue invoices forward to the week you realistically expect them, rather than counting them as cash</li>
  <li>track days sales outstanding (DSO) and update the forecast when it rises</li>
  <li>for subscription revenue, place annual and quarterly payments on the dates they bill, not spread evenly across the year</li>
  <li>keep pipeline separate from booked revenue until a deal is signed and invoiced</li>

</ul>

## What Is Automated Cash Flow Forecasting?


<p>Automated cash flow forecasting builds the forecast from connected systems instead of exports. The bank, the accounting system, and the billing or CRM system feed the forecast directly, so balances, open invoices, and bills update when they change.</p>


<p className="mb-2 font-medium text-foreground">Automation takes over:</p>


<ul>

</ul>


<p>It does not take over the judgment calls: which customers will pay late, which hires to make, and what cash floor is safe. Those stay with you as assumptions and scenarios.</p>

## What Are the Warning Signs of a Cash Flow Problem?


<p className="mb-2 font-medium text-foreground">A cash flow forecast is most useful when it warns you early. Watch for:</p>


<ul>

  <li>days sales outstanding rising for two or more months in a row</li>
